Tinge

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 23 November 2026

Tinge is a photo editor operated by George Leontiev and designed to work on your device. Your photographs are not uploaded to Tinge for editing.

Photos and edits

Tinge reads the photos you select and processes them on your device. When you save a copy, Tinge asks for permission to add it to Photos. If you choose to update an original, Tinge requests the Photos access needed to make a reversible edit; you can use Photos’ Revert feature to return to the original.

Exported images may retain metadata from the source, such as the date, camera information or location, and may include an editing note. Check a photo’s metadata before sharing it if that matters to you. If a selected photo is stored in iCloud, Apple Photos may download it to your device.

Sharing photos with Tinge

You can send photos to Tinge from Photos and other apps with the share sheet. Tinge’s share extension copies them into storage on your device that only Tinge can read, and Tinge deletes that copy once it has opened them. A photo opened with Open in is copied into Tinge’s storage by iOS and deleted once Tinge has read it. To avoid adding the same shared photo twice, Tinge keeps a fingerprint of each shared photo on your device (a hash, from which the photo can’t be recreated) alongside your recent edits. Shared photos and their fingerprints are not uploaded.

When you share edited photos from Tinge, it writes temporary copies for the app you choose and deletes them when the share sheet closes. That app’s own privacy practices apply to what you send it.

Information stored on your device

To let you resume editing offline, Tinge keeps a history of up to 500 recent edits on your device: each edit’s look, settings, a thumbnail and the Photos identifier of the original. It also keeps a local copy of the original photo for the most recent 12 edits that fit in 250 MB; older originals are read again from Photos when you open them. Tinge also stores your personal looks and preferences on your device. Local copies of originals are excluded from device backups; the recent-edit history and other app data may be included in your device or iCloud backup depending on your settings. Tinge never receives your backups.

Touch and hold a recent edit and choose Remove from Recents to remove its cached source and thumbnail. You can remove personal looks in Manage Looks. Deleting Tinge removes its local app data. Photos saved to your Photos library and files you exported elsewhere remain in those locations; backups may also retain data. You can change Photos permissions in your device’s Settings.

Preset catalogues and network requests

When Tinge starts, it requests its free preset catalogue over HTTPS from GitHub Pages. The request does not include your photographs, their metadata or your edit history. GitHub says that it logs and stores visitors’ IP addresses for security when GitHub Pages is accessed. GitHub handles this connection information under its privacy statement; its retention practices apply.

You can also choose to connect to another catalogue URL. That catalogue’s host receives your network request and its own privacy practices apply. Tinge saves a validated catalogue locally so installed looks and editing remain available offline. To replace a custom catalogue, choose Edit Custom URL… in the app’s catalogue options and then Connect, or open a local catalogue file. Tinge may still refresh its first-party catalogue at launch.

Optional account

You never need an account to edit photos, browse or install looks, or save. If you choose Sign in with Apple (About → Account), Tinge doesn’t ask Apple for your name or email. Apple gives Tinge an identifier for your Apple Account that is specific to Tinge’s developer. Tinge’s account service, hosted on Cloudflare, stores it linked to a random Tinge account ID, together with when you signed in, your sign-in sessions, and the tokens Apple issues so that Tinge can revoke Sign in with Apple when you delete your account. Your session is kept in your iPhone’s Keychain.

While you’re signed in, the looks you’ve saved or added (their settings, including any colour lookup table they contain) and which looks you’ve hidden, removed or reordered are stored with your account so they appear on your other devices. No photos, photo metadata, thumbnails or recent edits are sent. Requests to the account service include your IP address, which is used briefly to limit abuse and isn’t stored by Tinge; the service’s logs record only the kind of event, never your identifiers, tokens or IP address. Cloudflare handles connection information under its privacy policy.

You can sign out, or delete your account in About → Account, at any time. Deletion immediately removes your account, sessions, stored Apple tokens and the looks saved to your account; looks on your iPhone stay. Tinge also asks Apple to revoke Sign in with Apple for Tinge. If Apple can’t be reached, Tinge keeps only an encrypted copy of Apple’s token, not linked to you, and keeps trying for up to 7 days; you can also stop Sign in with Apple for Tinge yourself in Settings → Apple Account → Sign-In & Security → Sign in with Apple, which also deletes your account. Sign-in sessions expire after 30 days. Deleted data can remain in the hosting provider’s point-in-time recovery history for up to 30 days before it is overwritten; it isn’t used during that time.

Diagnostics

Tinge does not include a third-party analytics or crash-reporting SDK and does not send custom analytics events. It records limited, fixed diagnostic categories on your device when a photo import, save or catalogue refresh fails; these messages do not contain photos, file paths or the full error details.

Depending on your device settings, Apple may share app analytics or crash and performance reports with developers. You can manage analytics sharing in Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements. See Apple’s analytics information and Apple’s Privacy Policy.

Optional tips

If available, you can choose to leave an optional one-time tip through Apple’s in-app purchase system. Apple handles the transaction and payment information under its own terms and privacy practices; Tinge does not receive your payment card details. Tips do not unlock editing features and are not required to use or export your photos.

Contact and your choices

If you email us, we receive your email address and any information you choose to include. We use that information to respond and handle your request. If you have an account, you can delete it and the looks saved to it in the app at any time (see Optional account). You may ask about access to or deletion of information you have sent us by emailing tinge@g.omg.lol. We retain correspondence as needed to respond, maintain support records or meet legal obligations.

For information held by Apple, GitHub or another catalogue provider, their privacy policies explain their handling and available choices. Tinge does not control those providers’ retention of connection or transaction data.

We may update this policy as Tinge changes. We will publish the revised version at this address and update the date above. Review it when you update the app or use new features.
